Shardiya Navratri: The Tapasya of Maryada Purushottam

Scriptures across the Devi Bhagavata and Krittivasi Ramayana reveal that Shri Ram underwent rigorous tapasya during Shardiya Navratri to invoke Devi Durga's blessings, enduring righteous victory over the ten-headed monarch of Lanka.

According to the Devi Bhagavata and Krittivasi Ramayana, Bhagwan Shri Ram performed the Akalbodhan — an untimely invocation of Goddess Durga — in the autumn month of Ashwin, outside the traditional spring season of her worship. Witnessing his unconditional surrender and fierce resolve, Devi Durga blessed Shri Ram with the power and armory needed to vanquish Ravana.

This sacred nine-night tapasya is what lends Shardiya Navratri in Ayodhya its distinctive spiritual depth. It is not merely a festival of lights and celebration — it is the annual commemoration of Bhagwan Ram's victory of dharma over adharma, of truth over illusion, and of divine grace over dark sovereignty. The devout of Ayodhya observe these nine nights as a period of fasting, recitation of the Durga Saptashati, and devotion to both Shakti and Ram simultaneously.

Vijayadashami: The Consecration of Vijaya Muhurta

Celebrated on Ashwin Shukla Dashami, Vijayadashami marks the triumph of Shri Ram over Ravana. The entire city of Ayodhya transforms into a space of victory and spiritual festivities.

In Ayodhya, Vijayadashami transcends ordinary festivity. The Vijaya Muhurta — the supremely auspicious planetary alignment on Dashami — is observed by the city's military processions, new enterprise initiations, and the ancient custom of Shastra Puja (worship of weapons and tools). The Ram Janmbhoomi Mandir performs elaborate Abhishek and Darshan of Bhagwan Ram in his victorious form, while Ramlilas culminate with Ravana Vadh enacted by the city's foremost artists.

The Heritage Ramlila of Ayodhya Dham

In Ayodhya, Ramlila is not merely theatrical festival theatre. It is a sacred pilgrimage of consciousness. Celebrated across dozens of simultaneous stages, bringing together all dimensions of the great narrative — the entire community participates.

The Ramlila tradition in Ayodhya is believed to predate the Mughal era, maintained through oral transmission and living performance by families who have played the same divine roles for generations. Sankat Mochan's troupe at Ramkatha Park and the Ramleela at Saket Mahotsav ground draw lakhs of pilgrims and devotees from across India and the diaspora. Scholars from Banaras, Lucknow, and lakhnawi classical arts traditions have all enriched this living heritage.

During Navratri, performances run for nine consecutive nights, building in devotional intensity until the Dashami when the Ravana Dahan symbolically concludes the arc of dharmic struggle with the ultimate triumph of goodness.

The Eternal Throne: Sovereign Ram Darbar

Following the conquest of Adharma on Vijayadashami, Ayodhya's devotion prepares for the enthronement of Bhagwan Ram — the ceremony of sovereign Ram Darbar. Ayodhya's devotees enact the grand Darbar installation in the ambience of Ramkatha sessions, Maha Lalita Aarti, and Bhagwan Ram's throne — Simhasan — is ceremoniously decorated with Navaratna flowers and gold offerings, and Bhagwan Parasham reports his loyal supreme surrender.

Ram Katha: The Wisdom of Fortitude & Cosmic Surrender

Throughout the nine days of Navratri, Ayodhya invites the devout to Ram Katha sessions led by Bhagwatacharyaas — masters of the Ramcharitmanas — where Shri Ram's inner journey during Lanka Vijaya is meditated upon in depth. Devotees are drawn to understand how a dharmic king faced the most catastrophic challenges of existence — separation from Mata Sita, the army of adversity, and the weight of cosmic duty.

The Ramkatha sessions during Navratri specifically cover three themes, illuminating Shri Ram's extraordinary courage, surrender to Devi Shakti, and the surrender to Devi Shakti — each woven into the festival as living philosophy for contemporary devotees seeking guidance through the challenges of modern existence.

Moral Steadfastness in Calamity

Moral Steadfastness in Calamity

The Ramcharitmanas illuminates with extraordinary clarity how Shri Ram's acceptance of calamity as cosmic grace — never collapsing inward — transformed suffering into a source of infinite strength, generosity, and an inexhaustible spiritual authority as a compassionate king.

The Surrender to Devi Shakti

The Surrender to Devi Shakti

Understanding that true victory does not arise from pride or raw power but from divine surrender — Ram's invocation of Devi Durga through Akalbodhan is the supreme teaching of how the highest Purushottama, even as the Avatar, seeks the Mother's grace to accomplish dharmic purpose.

The Wisdom of Devi Shakti

The Wisdom of Devi Shakti

These understanding making Vijayadashami unique among Ram's festivals is the recognition that Bhagwan Ram's Sovereign Bhagwat Geeta too teaches — that the Sovereign Being transcends personal glory, destroying what must be destroyed not for self but for the restoration of cosmic Dharma, justice and eternal order.
