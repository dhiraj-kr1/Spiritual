The Origin of Garba

माँ अम्बा की आराधना से विश्वव्यापी उत्सव तक

Thousands of years ago, in the villages of Gujarat, women placed a clay pot with a burning lamp inside it and danced around it at night, singing to the goddess. That simple, luminous act became one of the world's greatest living cultural traditions.

01 - THE BEGINNING

It Began with a Clay Pot and a Flame

Before the music, before the costumes, before the stadiums and the thousands of dancers - there was a single clay pot with a lamp burning inside it, and women dancing around it in the dark.

🏺
🔥
गर्बो
The Sacred Clay Pot

The Garbo - The Sacred Vessel

The Garbo is a clay pot with small holes pierced through its walls. A lamp (diya) burns inside it. The light of the lamp shines through the holes like stars - this is the light of the goddess radiating through the vessel of the universe.

The pot represents the garbha - the womb of creation - and the lamp inside represents the divine light that sustains all life from within. Traditionally, the Garbo is placed on a bed of wheat or rice seeds, representing fertility and abundance.

Women would circle this pot, singing to the goddess, their movement a form of ritual worship - a moving prayer. This is the original Garba. Everything else grew from this single act of devotion.

02 - THE JOURNEY

From Village Ritual to World Stage

The story of Garba across the centuries - how it evolved, spread and eventually earned the world's highest cultural recognition.

Ancient Times

Shakti Worship in the Indus Valley

Archaeological evidence suggests that goddess worship and circular ritual dances were practised in the Indian subcontinent as far back as the Indus Valley Civilisation. Clay female figurines found at Mohenjo-daro and Harappa suggest that the veneration of the feminine divine - the foundation of Garba - has roots going back more than 4,000 years.

Vedic Period

The Concept of the Garbha Takes Shape

The Vedas and Upanishads develop the concept of the universe as a hiranyagarbha - the golden womb - from which all creation emerges. The goddess as the creative womb of the universe becomes a central theological concept. The ritual of placing a lamp-lit pot at the centre of worship finds its philosophical grounding here.

Medieval Gujarat · 900–1500 CE

Garba as Village Women's Worship

In medieval Gujarat, Garba became firmly established as a women's ritual during Navratri - performed in village squares and temple courtyards. Songs praising Maa Amba, Randal, Khodiyar and other local forms of the goddess were composed and passed down orally through generations of mothers and daughters. The dance was inseparable from the songs - each gesture mirrored each lyric.

15th–18th Century

The Bhakti Movement Deepens Garba

The Bhakti movement - which swept Gujarat through saint-poets like Narsinh Mehta - enriched Garba's spiritual content. Narsinh Mehta's devotional songs became part of the Garba repertoire. The dance began to absorb elements of Krishna devotion alongside goddess worship, leading eventually to Dandiya Raas being performed alongside Garba in the same Navratri celebrations.

19th–20th Century

From Village to City - Garba Goes Public

As Gujarat urbanised, Garba moved from village courtyards to city squares, and eventually to large grounds and stadiums. Community organisations (samaj) began organising public Garba events open to all. The dance that was once exclusively women's ritual became a community celebration. Men began participating. Music shifted from purely vocal to instrumental accompaniment.

Late 20th Century

The Gujarati Diaspora Takes Garba to the World

When millions of Gujaratis emigrated to the UK, USA, Canada, East Africa and beyond, they carried Garba with them. Navratri Garba events in Leicester, London, New Jersey, Houston and Nairobi became some of the largest outdoor cultural events in their respective cities. Garba had become a truly global tradition - while retaining its devotional Gujarati heart.

December 2023 ✦

UNESCO Recognition - Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity

At its 18th session in Kasane, Botswana, UNESCO's Intergovernmental Committee inscribed Garba of Gujarat on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The inscription recognised Garba's living role in fostering social cohesion, gender inclusivity, intergenerational transmission and devotional practice. For India, it was the 15th element inscribed on this UNESCO list.

03 - THE GODDESS

Maa Amba - The Goddess at Garba's Heart

While all forms of the goddess are celebrated during Navratri Garba, there is one form who is uniquely central to Gujarat's Garba tradition.

🌟

Maa Amba

The primary goddess of Gujarati Garba is Maa Amba (also called Ambaji) - a form of Shakti particularly venerated in Gujarat and Rajasthan. Her temple at Ambaji in the Aravalli hills is one of the 51 Shakti Peethas and draws millions of pilgrims during Navratri. Gujarat's Garba is performed in her honour above all others.

⚡

Maa Randal

Randal Mata is a beloved local form of the goddess particularly worshipped in Saurashtra (western Gujarat). Many traditional Garba songs are composed specifically in her praise. She is associated with lightning, fierce protection and the wild creative power of nature.

🐎

Maa Khodiyar

Khodiyar Mata - the crocodile-riding goddess - is one of Gujarat's most beloved local Shakti forms, particularly worshipped by the fishing and boat-building communities. She is celebrated in specific Garba songs that recount her miraculous birth story and her role as protector of her devotees.

🌸

Maa Bahuchara

Bahuchara Mata is the patron goddess of the Hijra (transgender) community in India and is especially venerated in central Gujarat. Her temple at Becharaji is one of Gujarat's most important pilgrimage sites, and she is praised in Garba songs for her unconditional acceptance of all who worship her.

04 - WHY IT SPREAD

Why Garba Captured the World

people

It Requires No Guru

Unlike classical Indian dance forms which require years of training, the basic Garba step can be learned in minutes. Anyone can join the circle. This democratic accessibility made Garba uniquely able to spread across communities and cultures.

favorite

It Is Genuinely Joyful

Garba produces a state of collective joy that is difficult to describe and impossible to resist. The music, the movement, the colours, the community - together they create an experience that draws people back year after year, whether or not they understand its devotional roots.

family_restroom

It Carries Memory

For Gujarati families separated from their homeland by migration, Garba carries the memory of home. Dancing it in a foreign city is an act of cultural preservation - of maintaining connection across generations and geographies. Garba became identity.

हिन्दी

गरबा की उत्पत्ति - एक मिट्टी के घड़े से विश्व विरासत तक

गरबा की उत्पत्ति गुजरात की प्राचीन शक्ति उपासना परम्परा से हुई है। "गरबा" शब्द संस्कृत के "गर्भ" से आया है। प्राचीन काल में गुजरात के गाँवों में नवरात्रि के दौरान महिलाएँ एक छिद्रयुक्त मिट्टी के घड़े (गर्बो) में दीपक जलाकर उसके चारों ओर गाते-नाचते हुए देवी की उपासना करती थीं।

मध्यकाल में नरसिंह मेहता जैसे सन्त-कवियों के भक्तिगीतों ने गरबे को और समृद्ध किया। 19वीं-20वीं शताब्दी में यह नृत्य गाँव से शहरों में आया और गुजराती प्रवासियों के साथ विश्वभर में फैला। अमेरिका, ब्रिटेन, कनाडा और अफ्रीका में लाखों लोग अब नवरात्रि पर गरबा करते हैं।

दिसम्बर 2023 में यूनेस्को ने गुजरात के गरबा को "मानवता की अमूर्त सांस्कृतिक विरासत" की सूची में शामिल किया - यह भारत की 15वीं प्रविष्टि थी इस प्रतिष्ठित सूची में।
