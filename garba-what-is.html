What is Garba?

गरबा - देवी की दिव्य परिक्रमा

Garba is not simply a folk dance. It is an act of worship - the devotee's body itself becoming the offering to the goddess. Danced in circles around a central lamp or the goddess's image, Garba is one of the oldest living forms of Shakti devotion in the world.

01 - THE ESSENCE

The Dance That Is a Prayer

In Garba, the body does not merely express devotion - the body IS the devotion. Every step, every clap, every turn is an act of worship directed at the goddess at the centre.

🌀

The Circle

Garba is always danced in a circle - never in lines or random formations. The circle represents the cycle of creation, preservation and dissolution. The goddess at the centre is the still point; the dancers are the cosmos revolving around her.

🪔

The Centre

At the centre of every Garba circle is either a diya (lamp), an image of the goddess, or the sacred Garbo (a clay pot with a lamp inside). The dance circles this light - the devotee always moving around the divine, never away from it.

👏

The Clap

The characteristic three claps of Garba are not simply rhythmic - they represent the three aspects of the goddess: Mahakali (creator of time), Mahalakshmi (sustainer) and Mahasarasvati (the liberator). Every clap is a salutation to one of her three faces.

🌙

The Night

Garba is danced at night - traditionally from dusk until dawn during the nine nights of Navratri. Night is the goddess's time: it is when she is most powerful, most accessible to prayer, and when the boundary between the devotee and the divine thins.

🏺

The Word "Garba" - What Does It Mean?

The word Garba comes from the Sanskrit Garbha (गर्भ) - meaning womb. The original Garba was not a dance at all, but a clay pot with holes pierced through it and a lamp burning inside - placed at the centre of worship to represent the womb of the universe and the creative power of the goddess who sustains all life.

Over time, the act of dancing around this sacred pot (called the Garbo) became itself a form of worship - and the dance took on the name of the vessel around which it revolved. The Garbo - the sacred pot - still sits at the centre of traditional Garba celebrations in Gujarat today.

02 - THE DANCE FORM

How Garba is Danced

Garba has no single fixed choreography - it is a living, breathing folk tradition with hundreds of regional variations. But certain elements are universal.

01

The Aarti Opening

Every Garba night begins not with dancing but with the Jay Adhyashakti aarti - a complete prayer to the goddess sung by all participants together, facing the central image or flame. Only after the aarti is complete does the Garba begin. The music is sacred before it is festive.

02

Forming the Circles

Participants form concentric circles around the centre - sometimes three, sometimes five, sometimes a single large circle depending on the venue. The innermost circle moves slowest; the outermost fastest. This mirrors the structure of the cosmos, planets orbiting a sun.

03

The Three-Step and Clap

The most basic Garba step is the be tali (two claps) or tran tali (three claps) - a rhythmic side-step with claps that everyone knows. As the night progresses and energy rises, the steps become more complex: the dodhiyu, chhand, and the spinning fuddi turns.

04

The Songs - Garba Geets

Traditional Garba songs (Garba Geets) are in Gujarati and praise the goddess in her various forms - Amba, Kali, Durga, Randal, Bahuchara. The songs alternate between slow, meditative invocations and fast, energetic praise choruses. The tempo of the dance follows the tempo of the song.

05

The Dandiya Transition

After Garba - usually in the second half of the night - Dandiya Raas begins. Participants pick up decorated sticks and the dance shifts from the goddess-worship of Garba to the playful battle-commemoration of Dandiya. The two are distinct but flow naturally into each other on Navratri nights.

04 - SPIRITUAL SIGNIFICANCE

Why Garba is More Than a Dance

self_improvement

The Body as Offering

In most forms of puja, the devotee offers external items - flowers, food, incense. In Garba, the devotee offers themselves - their energy, their breath, their movement. The dance is a yajna (sacred fire) in which the dancer is both the worshipper and the offering.

loop

The Cycle of Time

The circular movement of Garba represents the rotation of time - the cycles of day and night, birth and death, creation and dissolution. Dancing in a circle is an embodied meditation on the nature of existence: everything returns to the centre, the goddess.

groups

Community as Sangha

Garba is never danced alone. It requires a community. In this way it embodies the teaching that spiritual life is not a solitary endeavour - the goddess is found in the midst of community, in shared devotion, in the energy that arises when many hearts beat together in worship.

🏆
UNESCO Recognition 2023

Garba of Gujarat - Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity

In December 2023, UNESCO inscribed Garba of Gujarat on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity - recognising it as a living tradition of profound spiritual, cultural and communal importance. India's nomination highlighted Garba's role in fostering community solidarity, its connection to the worship of the goddess, and its transmission across generations within families and communities.

गरबा - देवी की परिक्रमा में जीवन का उत्सव

गरबा नवरात्रि का सबसे प्रमुख लोक नृत्य है जो मुख्यतः गुजरात में उद्भूत हुआ और अब समस्त भारत एवं विश्व में प्रसिद्ध है। "गरबा" शब्द संस्कृत के "गर्भ" से बना है - जिसका अर्थ है कोख या गर्भाशय। मूलतः यह एक मिट्टी के घड़े (गर्बो) के इर्द-गिर्द नृत्य था जिसमें दीपक जलाया जाता था - यह घड़ा देवी के गर्भ का प्रतीक था।

गरबा सदैव वृत्त (गोल) में किया जाता है - केन्द्र में देवी की प्रतिमा या दीपक होता है और भक्त उनके चारों ओर घूमते हुए नृत्य करते हैं। यह परिक्रमा की भाँति है - जिस प्रकार हम मंदिर में देवता की परिक्रमा करते हैं, उसी प्रकार गरबे में नृत्य द्वारा देवी की परिक्रमा की जाती है।

वर्ष 2023 में यूनेस्को ने गुजरात के गरबा को "मानवता की अमूर्त सांस्कृतिक विरासत" की सूची में शामिल किया - यह भारत और हिन्दू परम्परा के लिए गर्व का क्षण था।
